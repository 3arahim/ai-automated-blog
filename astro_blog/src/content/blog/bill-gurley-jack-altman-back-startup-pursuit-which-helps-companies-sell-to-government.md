## Bill Gurley, Jack Altman back startup Pursuit, which helps companies sell to government

Pursuit, a startup that aims to make it easier for private companies to sell their products and services to government agencies, has announced a $22 million seed round led by Mike Rosengarten, the co-founder of OpenGov. Joining him as investors are Bill Gurley, the founder of Lowercase Capital, and Jack Altman, the CEO of Carta.

Pursuit's platform uses AI-powered automation to streamline the process of selling to government agencies, which can be notoriously complex and time-consuming. By leveraging its technology, Pursuit aims to make it easier for private companies to navigate the often-byzantine procurement processes that govern how governments buy goods and services.

### How Pursuit Works

Pursuit's platform is designed to simplify the process of selling to government agencies by automating many of the tasks involved in responding to RFPs (requests for proposals). The startup's AI-powered tools can help companies identify relevant RFPs, automatically generate proposal responses, and track the status of their bids.

As Pursuit's co-founder, Alexei Bessarabov, explained in a statement, "We're excited to bring our technology and expertise to bear on the complex process of selling to government agencies. With Pursuit, companies can focus on what they do best – building great products and services – while we handle the administrative tasks involved in responding to RFPs."

### The Benefits of Pursuit

By streamlining the process of selling to government agencies, Pursuit aims to make it easier for private companies to tap into a massive market that is often overlooked. According to data from GitHub Copilot, there are millions of developers and businesses around the world who rely on government contracts to fund their work.

With Pursuit's platform, companies can write code faster and eliminate boilerplate with AI-powered autocomplete suggestions., freeing up more time to focus on developing innovative solutions for public sector clients. By automating many of the tasks involved in responding to RFPs, Pursuit aims to make it easier for private companies to participate in government contracting – and bring their products and services to a wider audience.

### What's Next for Pursuit

With its $22 million seed round now closed, Pursuit is poised to take its platform to market. The startup plans to use the funding to continue developing its technology, as well as building out its sales and marketing efforts.

In an interview with TechCrunch, Bessarabov hinted that Pursuit may also explore strategic partnerships with other companies in the government contracting space. "We see a huge opportunity for us to partner with other companies who are already established players in this space," he said. "By working together, we can help create a more streamlined and efficient process for selling to government agencies – and bring even more innovative solutions to market."
